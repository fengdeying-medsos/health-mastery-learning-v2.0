# Evidence and research guide

## Evidence questions

For each important claim, ask:
- What exactly is being claimed?
- In whom, under what conditions, and over what time?
- What outcome was measured?
- Is the evidence human, animal, cellular, or theoretical?
- Does the design support causation or only association?
- What are the effect size and uncertainty?
- What biases or confounders may matter?
- Does the evidence apply to the population in question?

## Avoid common reasoning errors

- Correlation is not automatically causation.
- A plausible mechanism is not proof of clinical benefit.
- Statistical significance is not the same as meaningful benefit.
- Relative risk reduction can obscure a small absolute effect.
- One study rarely settles a complex question.
- Absence of evidence is not always evidence of no effect.

## Reporting

State source type, date, population, design, findings, limitations, and relevance. If current research cannot be checked, say so plainly. Never fabricate citations or results.
